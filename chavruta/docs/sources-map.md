# Talmud & Halakha: A Map of the Sources

Sep 28, 2026 · @david raitan

## Orientation

Sort the shelf by what each work does, not by how famous its author is. The whole landscape in six lines:

- **Gemara, tier 1 (every daf):** Gemara, Rashi, Tosafot, plus the page's own tools — Mesoret HaShas, Ein Mishpat, the Bach and Gra glosses, Gilyon HaShas.
- **Gemara, tier 2 (standard iyun):** the Catalonian Rishonim — Ramban, Rashba, Ritva, Ran — read beside the Rif and the Rosh at the back of the volume.
- **Acharonim come in three kinds:** explainers (Maharsha, Maharam), question-raisers (Pnei Yehoshua, R' Akiva Eiger) and conceptual analysts (Ketzot and Netivot, then Brisk and R' Shimon Shkop).
- **Aggadah has its own ladder:** Rashi → Maharsha, the Ein Yaakov commentaries or Ben Yehoyada → Maharal → the thinkers.
- **Halakha is a chain** in which each work digests, sources or corrects the one before: Geonim → Rif → Rambam → Rosh → Tur → Beit Yosef → Shulchan Aruch + Rema → nosei kelim → your community's code.
- **The last rung depends on community:** Mishnah Berurah for most Ashkenazim in Orach Chaim; Yalkut Yosef or Halacha Berura in Rav Ovadia's line; Ben Ish Chai and Kaf HaChaim in the Iraqi and Kabbalah-weighted line; regional codes for Moroccan and Yemenite practice.

### How to read the tiers

- **A tier says how often you open a work, not how great its author was.** Tier 1 = every page. Tier 2 = the standard next step in iyun. Tier 2.5 = depends on the tractate. Tier 3 = specialist or lookup.
- **Genre predicts function.** An *explainer* tells you what the text says. A *question-raiser* tells you what problem you missed. An *analyst* tells you what the law is, conceptually. A *digest* tells you what survived as law. A *compendium* tells you who else said what. A *navigation tool* tells you where to look.
- **Every work sits on an earlier one.** Tosafot on Rashi, the Maharsha on both; the Ran on the Rif; the Kesef Mishneh on the Rambam; Beit Yosef on the Tur; the Magen Avraham on the Shulchan Aruch; the Mishnah Berurah on all of them. Know what a work sits on and you know most of what it does.

No yeshiva or authority publishes an official tier list. The tiers below are a synthesis of the printed page's own hierarchy, educators' guides, and how later works cite earlier ones; each tier table says so.

## Map 1 — Gemara: the printed page is already a tier chart

The Vilna (Romm) Shas, printed 1880–1886, keeps a layout inherited from the first complete Bomberg Talmud (Venice, 1520–1523). What surrounds the text is what every learner is expected to see; what sits at the back of the volume is the standard next step ([Hebrew Wikipedia, צורת הדף](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%93%D7%A3)).

Rashi always sits on the column toward the binding and Tosafot on the outer one; the extra early commentaries in the margin change from tractate to tractate.

Two corrections to common assumptions:

- **Mesoret HaShas is not by R' Yehoshua Boaz.** It first appeared anonymously in Salonika in 1523. Boaz adapted it to the Venice pagination and wrote Ein Mishpat, Ner Mitzvah and Torah Or himself ([Genauer, Hakirah 31](https://hakirah.org/Vol31Genauer.pdf)).
- **"Rashi" is not always Rashi.** Rashbam carries Bava Batra from 29a and Rivan carries Makkot from 19b. The commentary printed as Rashi on Nedarim and Nazir comes from the Mainz school, so in Nedarim learners lean on the Ran and the Rosh ([Hebrew Wikipedia, פירוש רש"י לתלמוד](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A9_%D7%A8%D7%A9%22%D7%99_%D7%9C%D7%AA%D7%9C%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%93); [Sefaria, Ran on Nedarim](https://www.sefaria.org/Ran_on_Nedarim)).

## Rashi explains locally; Tosafot read Shas as one book

The first split between actor types is Rashi against Tosafot. Rashi explains each passage where it stands, briefly and precisely, and does not try to square it with other tractates — so his reading of one phrase can differ between masechtot ([Hebrew Wikipedia, פירוש רש"י לתלמוד](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A9_%D7%A8%D7%A9%22%D7%99_%D7%9C%D7%AA%D7%9C%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%93)). Tosafot start from the opposite premise: the whole Talmud is one consistent work, so a contradiction between sugyot must be resolved by a distinction ([Hebrew Wikipedia, תוספות](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%AA)).

- **Their signature move** is *ve-im tomar… ve-yesh lomar* — "and if you ask… one may answer".
- **Most of their material** traces back to two masters, Rabbeinu Tam and the Ri HaZaken ([JTS Library guide](https://www.jtsa.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Medieval_Commentary_Responsa_and_Codes_L-1.pdf)).
- **What a Tosafot-style reader asks:** Does this contradict another sugya or plain logic? Is Rashi's reading enough? Why did the Gemara need to say something derivable elsewhere? How does accepted practice fit the text?
- **A caution:** the printed Tosafot are a patchwork of collections (Touques, Sens, Evreux and others), chosen tractate by tractate by the first printers. "Asking from one Tosafot to another" only works where you know it is the same collection. When a Tosafot is terse, Tosafot HaRosh often shows what it meant ([DafYomi.co.il](https://dafyomi.co.il/rhashanah/rh-in-biblio.htm)).

### Each school of Rishonim specialized in a genre

- **Ashkenaz (France and Germany):** the running explanation (Rashi), dialectical glosses (Tosafot), and halakhic compendia (Rosh, Mordechai, Or Zarua).
- **Provence:** critique and defense of the Rif — the Ba'al HaMaor against it, the Raavad and the Ramban answering him — plus the Meiri's summarizing digest.
- **Catalonia:** *chiddushim*, analytic novellae that fuse the Andalusian halakhic tradition with Tosafist dialectic. The line runs Ramban → Rashba and Ra'ah → Ritva and Ran ([Leibowitz, Torah Musings](https://www.torahmusings.com/2018/05/later-rishonim-iv-catalonia/)). This is the working second tier in yeshivot today.
- **Italy:** Tosafot Rid and Piskei Riaz.

Three works need their own label:

- **Meiri (Beit HaBechirah):** skips the give-and-take and states the upshot, naming Rishonim by epithet. It was largely unknown until the full Parma manuscript surfaced in 1920. Excellent as an overview; light as an authority in psak ([Wikipedia, Menachem Meiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Meiri)).
- **Shita Mekubetzet (R' Betzalel Ashkenazi, 16th c.):** a compendium that is sometimes the only surviving source for lost Rishonim ([Hebrew Wikipedia, שיטה מקובצת](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%98%D7%94_%D7%9E%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%91%D7%A6%D7%AA)).
- **Rabbeinu Chananel (Kairouan, 11th c.):** the earliest surviving systematic commentary, a summary-paraphrase carrying Geonic readings and Yerushalmi parallels. It is most useful as an independent witness to the text, since Rashi's students often corrected manuscripts to match his readings.

## Acharonim: explainers, question-raisers, conceptualists

The later commentators on the Gemara do four different jobs, and the job — not the name — tells you when to open them.

1. **Emenders fix the printed text.** Maharshal's *Chochmat Shlomo* (an abridgment sits in nearly every edition), then the Bach, the Ya'avetz, the Gra and the Rashash ([Wikipedia, Solomon Luria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Luria)).
2. **Explainers (מפרשים) say what Rashi and Tosafot mean, and why.** The Maharsha is the anchor; the old saying is that whoever has grasped the Maharsha has understood Tosafot ([Wikipedia, Maharsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharsha)). The Maharam Lublin is more straightforward; the Maharam Schiff is terser and harder.
3. **Question-raisers (בעלי קושיות וחידושים) show you the problem you missed.** The Pnei Yehoshua is the classic. R' Akiva Eiger's short *tzarich iyun* questions and cross-references tie distant sugyot together, and his method has aged far better than the pilpul of his era ([Wikipedia, Akiva Eiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiva_Eiger)). The Tzlach, Hafla'ah, Chatam Sofer, Aruch LaNer and Sfat Emet belong here too, each on some tractates only.
4. **Conceptual analysts (למדנות) ask what a law *is*.** Its proto-texts are two Shulchan Aruch commentaries that work as Gemara books: the Ketzot HaChoshen and the Netivot HaMishpat that argues with it, studied as a pair ([Wikipedia, Ketzos HaChoshen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketzos_HaChoshen)). R' Chaim Soloveitchik's Brisker method then defines each concept precisely, with tools like *gavra/cheftza*, *ma'aseh/kiyum*, *tzvei dinim* and the *chakira*; each distinction must make a practical difference ([Wikipedia, Brisker method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisker_method)). R' Shimon Shkop's *Sha'arei Yosher* works on the principles that generate the law. The Chazon Ish is the recognized Gemara-first counterweight to Brisk.

**On the word "pilpul".** Historically, pilpul was the 15th–16th-century Polish method of ingenious distinctions (R' Yaakov Pollak, R' Shalom Shachna). The Maharal, the Shelah, the Chavot Yair and the Gra all attacked it ([Jewish Virtual Library, Pilpul](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/pilpul)). R' Akiva Eiger, the Chatam Sofer and Brisk saw themselves as its opposite. The precise labels are *question-and-novellae writers* for the 18th–19th-century classics, and *lomdus* for Ketzot → Brisk → Telz.

**The Sephardi iyun tradition.** R' Yitzchak Canpanton (Castile, 1360–1463) taught a close-reading method for Gemara, Rashi and Tosafot: nothing in their language is missing or superfluous, so you ask why each word is there, what wrong reasoning it blocks, and where the heart of the answer lies. It survives as "Iyyun Tunisai" at R' Meir Mazuz's Kisse Rahamim ([Seforim Blog](https://seforimblog.com/2014/02/a-third-way-iyyun-tunisai-as/?print=print)). Porat Yosef built the Israeli Sephardi yeshiva model on Talmud together with the poskim and the Shulchan Aruch commentaries ([Wikipedia, Porat Yosef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porat_Yosef_Yeshiva)). Many Sephardi students now learn in Litvish yeshivot, and one Hebrew essay argues for Sephardi close reading first and Litvish abstraction after ([daat.ac.il](https://www.daat.ac.il/daat/kitveyet/shana/ravana-1.htm)).

## Aggadah runs on its own ladder

For an aggadic passage, open Rashi and the Maharsha first, then an Ein Yaakov commentary or Ben Yehoyada, and only then the Maharal. The halakhic commentators mostly fall silent here — the Rif leaves aggadah out of his digest entirely ([Wikipedia, Isaac Alfasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Alfasi)).

### Two frames for how to read it

- **The Geonic–Maimonidean frame:** aggadah is not binding and is often figurative. The Rambam's introduction to Perek Chelek sorts readers into three groups and praises the few who understand that the Sages spoke in riddle and parable ([Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Rambam_on_Mishnah_Sanhedrin.10.1)). R' Avraham ben HaRambam classifies the kinds of derashot, and the Mevo HaTalmud tells you to take from aggadah only what reason supports.
- **The Maharal's frame:** nothing in an aggadah was said for effect; every detail encodes an idea about reality (*Be'er HaGolah*). Critics and admirers alike call this a revolution that now dominates the yeshiva world ([Etzion, Eitam](https://etzion.org.il/en/philosophy/great-thinkers/maharal/darkei-parshanut-haagada-yachaso-shel-haMaharal-laRambam); [Eisen, Hakirah 4](https://hakirah.org/Vol%204%20Eisen.pdf)).
- **Between them, the Ramchal's toolkit** (*Ma'amar al HaHaggadot*, 1736): the Sages concealed meaning through metaphor, through unstated conditions, through depth hidden in trivial-looking details, and through the science of their day used as a garment ([Daat](https://www.daat.ac.il/daat/mahshevt/agadot/hagada2-2.htm)).

### Who does what

- **Maharsha, Chiddushei Aggadot** — the first stop after Rashi, and the only systematic aggadah commentary printed in every standard Shas. He draws on philosophy and Kabbalah and reads the strange tales as parables ([Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Chidushei_Agadot_on_Berakhot)).
- **Ein Yaakov** (R' Yaakov ibn Chaviv, first printed 1516) gathers the Bavli's aggadot with a stack of commentaries that divide the labor. R' Chanoch Zundel of Bialystok wrote three: *Etz Yosef* for plain meaning, *Anaf Yosef* for homiletics, *Yad Yosef* for parallel sources ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enoch_Zundel_ben_Joseph)). Beside them: *HaKotev* by the compiler himself, R' Yaakov Reischer's *Iyun Yaakov*, and the "Rif" — who is R' Yoshiyahu Pinto, not Alfasi.
- **Ben Yehoyada** (the Ben Ish Chai) — for Sephardi and Mizrahi learners, the major commentary on the Bavli's aggadot. It works on the plain level and on the levels of *remez* and *sod*, and it grew out of the daily Ein Yaakov class after Shacharit in Baghdad ([Sefaria, introduction](https://www.sefaria.org/Ben_Yehoyada_on_Berakhot,_Introduction)). *Benayahu* is its shorter companion.
- **Maharal** — "next level" because he reveals the idea behind each element rather than solving the textual difficulty ([Etzion, Eitam](https://etzion.org.il/en/philosophy/great-thinkers/maharal/darko-shel-hamaharal-bebiurei-aggadot-chazal)). His fullest treatment often sits in a thematic book: *Netzach Yisrael* on exile and redemption, *Tiferet Yisrael* on Torah, *Netivot Olam* on character.
- **Thinkers reached by topic, not by page:** Rav Kook's *Ein Ayah* (Berakhot and most of Shabbat only), R' Tzadok, the Sfat Emet, R' Hutner's *Pachad Yitzchak*, R' Moshe Shapiro ([Etzion, Blau](https://www.etzion.org.il/en/talmud/studies-gemara/midrash-and-aggada/understanding-aggada-introduction)).
- **The academic track:** Yonah Fraenkel reads each story as a closed literary unit; Jeffrey Rubenstein reads it in its sugya as the redactors' composition ([Ancient Jew Review](https://www.ancientjewreview.com/read/2016/2/9/talmudicstories)); Binyamin Lau's *The Sages* supplies the historical setting.

A test worth keeping, from Yitzchak Blau, before reading a detail symbolically: is the detail *necessary* to the plot, and does the symbolic reading *resonate* with wider Torah themes ([Etzion, Blau](https://etzion.org.il/en/talmud/studies-gemara/midrash-and-aggada/meaning-details-aggadic-methodology))?

## Map 2 — Halakha: a chain that digests, sources and corrects itself

Every halakhic work answers the one before it: a digest compresses the Gemara, a code rules, a commentary supplies the sources, a critique challenges, a gloss adds another community's practice. The Encyclopaedia Judaica traces the same pattern of disagreement, defense and interpretation around the Rif, then the Rambam, the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch ([EJ, Codification of Law](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/codification-law)).

| Digest of the Gemara (ספר הלכות) | Keeps the Talmud's own words, drops what doesn't bear on practice, in tractate order | Halachot Gedolot (הלכות גדולות), Rif, Rosh, Mordechai |

| Synthesizing "last word" | Surveys the later authorities and states the practical conclusion | Mishnah Berurah (משנה ברורה), Aruch HaShulchan (ערוך השולחן), Kaf HaChaim (כף החיים), Yalkut Yosef (ילקוט יוסף), Halacha Berura (הלכה ברורה) |

### From the Geonim to the three pillars and the Tur

- **The Geonim (c. 589–1038)** wrote the first books of halakhot — She'iltot, Halachot Pesukot, Halachot Gedolot — and set the precedent of leaving out laws not practiced in their day. For you they are upstream data, met when a Rishon quotes "the Geonim" or in B.M. Lewin's *Otzar HaGeonim*. The Rambam wrote that the Rif's work made all of them unnecessary ([Sefaria, Introduction to the Mishnah](https://www.sefaria.org/Introduction_to_the_Mishnah)).
- **The Rif (1013–1103)** is a digest in the Talmud's own words, nicknamed *Talmud Katan*, covering the tractates in practice plus Berakhot and Chullin. Where sugyot conflict he picks one, where Tosafot would reconcile them. The Ran and Nimukei Yosef explain him, the Ba'al HaMaor attacks him, and the Ramban's *Milchamot* defends him ([Hebrew Wikipedia, הלכות רב אלפס](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%94%D7%9C%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%A8%D7%91_%D7%90%D7%9C%D7%A4%D7%A1)).
- **The Rambam's Mishneh Torah (completed c. 1178)** is the only code of all Jewish law: 14 books, Mishnaic Hebrew, one ruling per point, no names or proofs. That design provoked the Raavad's famous gloss, *סבר לתקן ולא תקן* ("he meant to improve and did not"), and the Rosh's warning not to rule from the book without finding its support in the Gemara. So the Rambam is always read with his nosei kelim (table below).
- **The Rosh (d. 1327)** brought Tosafist learning from Germany to Toledo. His *Piskei HaRosh* follows the Rif's model but adds Tosafist comments, which makes him the Ashkenazi voice among the three pillars ([Hebrew Wikipedia, פסקי הרא"ש](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%A7%D7%99_%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%90%22%D7%A9)).
- **The Tur (R' Yaakov ben Asher, the Rosh's son)** created the four sections every later code uses — Orach Chaim (daily life, prayer, Shabbat, festivals), Yoreh De'ah (kashrut, niddah, mourning and more), Even HaEzer (marriage and divorce), Choshen Mishpat (civil law) — with numbered simanim. It states each rule briefly and then surveys the opinions, so it is a code and a map at once.

| Rambam commentator | Genre | What it's good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hasagot HaRaavad (השגות הראב"ד) | Critique | The main challenge; where he is silent, he agrees |
| Maggid Mishneh (מגיד משנה) | Sources + defense | The most important source commentary; survives on only some books |
| Kesef Mishneh (כסף משנה) | Sources + defense | By Karo himself; shows why Maran sides with the Rambam |
| Hagahot Maimoniyot (הגהות מיימוניות) | Ashkenazi supplement | Tosafist rulings and customs the Rambam lacks |
| Migdal Oz, Radbaz, Lechem Mishneh (מגדל עוז, רדב"ז, לחם משנה) | Defense, harmonization | Lechem Mishneh targets apparent contradictions with the Talmud |
| Mishneh LaMelech (משנה למלך) | Analytic | Deep treatment of hard halakhot |
| Or Sameach, R' Chaim, Even HaAzel, Avi Ezri (אור שמח, חידושי רבנו חיים הלוי, אבן האזל) | Lomdus | Serve the Gemara track more than practical psak |
| R' Yosef Kafih; Yad Peshuta | Modern edition, commentary | Kafih restores the Yemenite text |

## Beit Yosef counts pillars; the Rema counts generations

Karo decided by counting the early giants; the Rema decided by weighing the later Ashkenazi authorities and custom. That one difference explains most Sephardi–Ashkenazi splits, which often reduce to "Rif and Rambam against Tosafot and Rosh".

- **Beit Yosef (begun 1522, completed 1542)** is Karo's source encyclopedia on the Tur, and each siman ends in a ruling. He built it on the Tur, not the Rambam, because the Tur gathers the opinions while the Rambam gives only one ([Sefaria, Beit Yosef introduction](https://www.sefaria.org/Beit_Yosef,_Introduction)).
- **His decision rule** (same introduction): where two of the three pillars — Rif, Rambam, Rosh — agree, the law follows them, unless most sages disagree and custom went the other way. If one pillar is silent and the other two split, he follows the majority of Ramban, Rashba, Ran, Mordechai and SMaG. A local custom to be stringent stands even against the count.
- **It is a counting rule, not a reasoning rule.** Karo declined to decide by Talmudic argument, since every view comes with proofs. Because the Rambam usually follows the Rif, Maran often lands with the Rambam. Where the Talmud is not explicit, he will not rule against the Zohar — hence no tefillin on Chol HaMoed ([Beit Yosef OC 31](https://www.sefaria.org/Beit_Yosef,_Orach_Chayim.31.2)).
- **The Rema's Darkei Moshe** answers with *hilkheta kebatrai*: rule like the later authorities, which brings in the Tosafist-trained Ashkenazi poskim and minhag ([Hebrew Wikipedia, המפה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%A4%D7%94)). The **Bach** traced every law in the Tur to its source, decided independently, and criticized those who relied on the Shulchan Aruch alone.
- **The Shulchan Aruch (completed 1563, printed Venice 1564–65)** is Karo's own synopsis of Beit Yosef's rulings — no sources, no names — laid out for review every month. The **Rema's glosses** (the Mapah, marked הגה) add Ashkenazi rulings and custom. They supplement more than they attack, and they are what opened the way for Ashkenazi acceptance of the code ([EJ, Shulḥan Arukh](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/shulhan-arukh)).
- **Acceptance came slowly.** The Maharshal, the Maharal, the Maharam Lublin and the Bach opposed ruling from a code. It became *the* code only once the 17th-century nosei kelim were written on it. The standing formula since: Sephardim follow Maran, Ashkenazim follow the Rema.

### The Ashkenazi track: canonical pairs, then the Mishnah Berurah

Each section of the Shulchan Aruch has a canonical pair of 17th-century commentators with a traditional nickname ([Halachipedia, Klalei HaPsak](https://www.halachipedia.com/index.php?title=Klalei_HaPsak_%28Halachic_Decision_Making%29)). In Orach Chaim the Magen Avraham usually prevails over the Taz, and in Yoreh De'ah the Shach over the Taz — a tendency reported in R' Yosef Dov Soloveitchik's name, not a binding rule.

| Section | Primary voices (the pair) | Explainers of the commentators | Digests and finding aids (tools, not voices) | Later weight-bearers and codes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orach Chaim (אורח חיים) | Magen Avraham (מגן אברהם), Taz (ט"ז) — "Maginei Eretz" | Pri Megadim (פרי מגדים), Machatzit HaShekel (מחצית השקל) | Be'er Heitev (באר היטב), Sha'arei Teshuvah (שערי תשובה), Be'er HaGolah | Beur HaGra, Eliyah Rabbah, Chayei Adam (חיי אדם), Shulchan Aruch HaRav (שולחן ערוך הרב), **Mishnah Berurah**, Aruch HaShulchan |
| Yoreh De'ah (יורה דעה) | Shach (ש"ך), Taz — "Ashlei Ravrevei" | Pri Megadim (שפתי דעת) | Pitchei Teshuvah (פתחי תשובה), Darkei Teshuvah (דרכי תשובה) | Chavot Da'at (חוות דעת), R' Akiva Eiger, Chochmat Adam (חכמת אדם), Aruch HaShulchan, Badei HaShulchan |
| Even HaEzer (אבן העזר) | Chelkat Mechokek (חלקת מחוקק), Beit Shmuel (בית שמואל) | — | Be'er Heitev, Pitchei Teshuvah, Otzar HaPoskim (אוצר הפוסקים) | Avnei Miluim; the responsa literature |
| Choshen Mishpat (חושן משפט) | Sma (סמ"ע), Shach | — | Be'er Heitev, Pitchei Teshuvah | Ketzot HaChoshen, Netivot HaMishpat, Urim VeTumim, Aruch HaShulchan |

*The pairs and nicknames follow Halachipedia; the split into voices, explainers and tools is this map's synthesis.*

- **Digests point to the voices; they are not voices.** The Chafetz Chaim himself describes the Be'er Heitev as a collection and abridgment of the Taz and Magen Avraham, and warns that abridging breeds errors ([Mishnah Berurah, introduction](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Berurah%2C_Introduction)). Treat Sha'arei Teshuvah and Pitchei Teshuvah (digests of responsa) the same way.
- **The Mishnah Berurah (R' Yisrael Meir Kagan, the Chafetz Chaim, 1884–1907)** replaced that digest layer for Orach Chaim. The main text gives the later authorities' practical conclusion, Sha'ar HaTziyun gives the sources, Beur Halacha the extended analysis. Where two Acharonim disagree he surveys the rest to see where most lean, uses the Gra as a tiebreaker, and avoids reflexive stringency, since a stringency in one place can become a leniency in another (same introduction).
- **Its standing:** it has largely displaced the Chayei Adam and the Aruch HaShulchan as the Ashkenazi authority for daily life ([Wikipedia, Mishnah Berurah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishnah_Berurah)). Gil Student's caution still holds: it is the best starting point, not the final word on every question ([Torah Musings](https://www.torahmusings.com/2006/11/mishnah-berurah-reaches-important/)).
- **The Aruch HaShulchan (R' Yechiel Michel Epstein)** covers all four sections, re-derives each siman from the Talmud and Rambam, and treats accepted practice as evidence where the Mishnah Berurah leans on texts ([Aspaqlaria](https://aspaqlaria.aishdas.org/2021/11/29/reconsidering-the-aruch-hashulchan/)). R' Y.E. Henkin preferred it, and R' Moshe Feinstein reportedly did too. Its Orach Chaim was written alongside the MB, so "it's later" is a weak argument there ([Seforim Blog](https://seforimblog.com/2019/05/arukh-hashulhan/)).
- **Why the MB won,** per Haym Soloveitchik's "Rupture and Reconstruction": a shift from practice learned at home to practice squared with the written text ([PDF](https://www.bjpa.org/content/upload/bjpa/rupt/RuptureAndReconstruction.pdf)).
- **Who follows what:** Chabad follows Shulchan Aruch HaRav. Bnei Brak Litvaks let the Chazon Ish override the MB on measurements, eruvin and shemittah ([Wikipedia, Chazon Ish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chazon_Ish)). Religious Zionists largely use R' Eliezer Melamed's Peninei Halakha, which follows the majority of poskim, gives custom real weight, and records each community's practice separately ([Peninei Halakha](https://ph.yhb.org.il/12-00-00/)). The Kitzur Shulchan Aruch (R' Shlomo Ganzfried, 1864) is a handbook, not a basis for rulings.
- **The modern layer:** the great responsa (Noda BiYehuda, Chatam Sofer, R' Akiva Eiger) serve as precedents; the 20th-century gedolim — Igrot Moshe, R' Shlomo Zalman Auerbach, R' Elyashiv, Shevet HaLevi — bind their own communities. Shemirat Shabbat KeHilchata generally follows R' Auerbach. Piskei Teshuvot is a useful digest on the MB's order, but reviewers found misquotations, so check what it cites ([Seforim Blog](https://seforimblog.com/2007/06/review-new-volume-of-peskei-teshuva-on/)).

## The Sephardi track: one Maran, five schools

Every Sephardi and Mizrahi school puts Maran — the Shulchan Aruch read with Beit Yosef — at the base. They differ on what may override him: the Arizal and Kabbalah, a community's older custom, the Rambam, or a posek's own reasoning.

**Kabbalat hora'ot Maran.** The doctrine holds that Karo's ruling decides even against a majority of poskim. It rests on two ideas: the halakha was settled by communal acceptance (*ipsika hilkheta*), and Maran is the recognized authority of the place (*mara de-atra*). Its main support is the Chida's report that about two hundred rabbis of Karo's generation endorsed his method ([Hebrew Wikipedia, קבלת הוראות מרן](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%9F)).

It was never absolute. Acceptance in Eretz Yisrael came gradually — the Chida wrote that in his own day Maran was not yet the mara de-atra — and one scholar counted more than a thousand places where even adherents depart from Maran (same article).

### Between Maran and today

- **Knesset HaGedolah** (R' Chaim Benveniste) gathers the first century of rulings after the Shulchan Aruch.
- **Pri Chadash** (R' Chizkiyah da Silva) is an independent critic of Maran and the Rema. The Cairo rabbinate first banned it; it was later accepted and printed in the standard editions ([Hebrew Wikipedia, פרי חדש](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%99_%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9)).
- **The Chida** (R' Chaim Yosef David Azulai): his *Birkei Yosef* is one of the few works on all four sections of the Shulchan Aruch, and later poskim lean on it heavily ([Hebrew Wikipedia, ברכי יוסף](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%99_%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%A3)). He is common ground for every Maran-based school.
- **Regional works** carry local weight: Mateh Yehuda for Algiers, Shulchan Gavoah for Salonika, Erech HaShulchan for Tunisia.
- **Watch the name:** two books are called Kaf HaChaim. R' Chaim Palagi's (Izmir) is an earlier work of halakha and musar on the daily order; R' Yaakov Chaim Sofer's is the encyclopedic commentary, seif by seif.

### The five schools

1. **Ben Ish Chai – Kaf HaChaim – Or LeTzion.** The Ben Ish Chai (R' Yosef Chaim of Baghdad, d. 1909) arranges practical halakhot by weekly parasha over two years of sermons. He accepts Maran in principle but often rules against him, usually more strictly, and brings in the Arizal and the Rashash even where they contradict the Shulchan Aruch ([Hebrew Wikipedia, בן איש חי](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%91%D7%9F_%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A9_%D7%97%D7%99_%28%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%A8%29)). It is binding practice above all for Iraqi Jews; his responsa are *Rav Pe'alim*.
   - R' Yaakov Chaim Sofer's **Kaf HaChaim** (from 1910; Orach Chaim, and Yoreh De'ah to siman 119) resembles the Mishnah Berurah in layout but gathers far more views and tends to decide by Kabbalah ([Hebrew Wikipedia, כף החיים](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%9B%D7%A3_%D7%94%D7%97%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D)). "The Sephardi Mishnah Berurah" describes its role, not its method.
   - Later carriers: R' Ben-Zion Abba Shaul's **Or LeTzion**, which holds that where a custom exists Maran was not accepted, while the Eastern communities accepted the Arizal outright ([Hebrew Wikipedia, אור לציון](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%90%D7%95%D7%A8_%D7%9C%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9F)); and **R' Mordechai Eliyahu**, who follows the Zohar wherever the Gemara does not explicitly contradict it.
2. **Rav Ovadia Yosef (1920–2013): "restoring the crown".** He installed Maran as the mara de-atra of the Land of Israel ([Hebrew Wikipedia, עובדיה יוסף](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93%D7%99%D7%94_%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%A3)). His meta-rules: Maran over the Arizal; *safek berachot lehakel* even against the Arizal; prefer the sound leniency (*koach de-hetera adif*), since piling up stringencies erodes the core; and Eastern communities in Israel drop diaspora customs that conflict with Maran.
   - Works: *Yabia Omer*, *Yechaveh Da'at*, *Chazon Ovadia*, and *Halichot Olam* — his glosses on the Ben Ish Chai, whom he disputes in more than a hundred places ([halachayomit.co.il](https://halachayomit.co.il/en/pdf/5927.pdf)).
   - Codified downstream: **Yalkut Yosef** (R' Yitzchak Yosef; a first digest in 1971, the full series from 1985), a ruling-first code in the Shulchan Aruch's order with a one- or two-volume Kitzur ([Hebrew Wikipedia, ילקוט יוסף](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%98_%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%A3)); and **Halacha Berura** (R' David Yosef), which copies the Mishnah Berurah's three-layer design and covers most of Orach Chaim.
   - This line holds the Chief Rabbinate: R' Shlomo Amar (2003–2013), R' Yitzchak Yosef (2013–2024), and R' David Yosef, elected 29 September 2024 ([Wikipedia, David Yosef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Yosef)).
3. **Moroccan minhag.** R' Shalom Messas (*Shemesh uMagen*) held that the acceptance binds only the Shulchan Aruch and Beit Yosef as written, and that prior Moroccan custom comes first; this led to a sharp dispute with Rav Ovadia ([Hebrew Wikipedia, שלום משאש](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A9%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%9D_%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%90%D7%A9)). His cousin R' Yosef Messas represents a bolder, times-have-changed line ([jewishideas.org, Shapiro](https://www.jewishideas.org/article/rabbi-joseph-messas)).
4. **Yemenite.** Three sub-rites: Dor De'ah and R' Yosef Kafih follow the Rambam; the Baladi follow the Rambam plus the Maharitz's custom, with Maran as fallback (R' Yitzchak Ratzabi's *Shulchan Aruch HaMekutzar*); the Shami accept the Shulchan Aruch ([Jewish Link, Jachter](https://jewishlink.news/a-brief-sketch-of-yemenite-halachic-history/)).
5. **Uziel / HaLevi.** R' Ben-Zion Meir Hai Uziel (*Mishpetei Uziel*; the State of Israel's first Sephardi Chief Rabbi) and R' Chaim David HaLevi (*Aseh Lecha Rav*; *Kitzur Shulchan Aruch Mekor Chaim*, the main halakha textbook in state-religious schools). Maran-based, and willing to reason halakha toward the realities of a Jewish state and modern life ([Hebrew Wikipedia, חיים דוד הלוי](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%93%D7%95%D7%93_%D7%94%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%99)).

| School | Base | What can override Maran | Everyday code | Typical followers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rav Ovadia (מרן הרב עובדיה) | Shulchan Aruch + Beit Yosef; the Chida | Almost nothing; only old custom predating Maran | Yalkut Yosef (ילקוט יוסף) or its Kitzur; Halacha Berura (הלכה ברורה) | Much of the traditional Sephardi public; Shas-aligned communities; the Chief Rabbinate |

## Six rules of weight connect the layers

A handful of named rules decide who outweighs whom. Two pull in opposite directions — later authorities win, yet later generations defer to earlier ones — and communal acceptance settles the rest.

| Rule | What it says | Source and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hilkheta kebatrai (הלכתא כבתראי) | The law follows the later authorities | A Geonic rule, not a Talmudic one, applied from Abaye and Rava onward; it also explains why the Bavli prevails over the Yerushalmi. The Rema codifies it for poskim, except where the later ones may not have seen an obscure earlier source ([Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%94%D7%9C%D7%9B%D7%94_%D7%9B%D7%91%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%99); [SA CM 25:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh,_Choshen_Mishpat.25.2)) |
| Deference across eras | Later generations do not dispute earlier ones | The Kesef Mishneh explains that after the Mishnah and Gemara were sealed, later sages accepted not to dispute them ([Mamrim 2:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Kessef_Mishneh_on_Mishneh_Torah,_Rebels.2.1)). "Acharonim don't argue with Rishonim" is a widely held norm, not formal halakha |
| Communal acceptance | Maran binds Sephardim; the Rema binds Ashkenazim | Kabbalat hora'ot Maran; the Ashkenazi acceptance of the Mapah and its commentators |
| Majority and doubt | Follow the many over the one; a doubt in Torah law goes strict, a rabbinic doubt goes lenient; in money, the holder keeps it | Codified in the Rema at CM 25:2, which also bars "I'll rule like whoever I like" unless one can decide by proofs |
| Minhag (מנהג) | Established custom stands even against the count | Written into Beit Yosef's own rule; central for Moroccan poskim and in Peninei Halakha |
| Ein lomdin halakha mipi talmud | You don't learn practical halakha from a study discussion until told it is the ruling for practice | [Bava Batra 130b](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.130b) |

Two opposite safeguards run through the whole history:

- **Don't rule from the Gemara's discussion alone** — Bava Batra 130b; and Ibn Migash held that no one in his day knew the Talmud well enough to rule from it directly.
- **Don't rule from a code alone** — Paltoi Gaon warned about the Geonic digests, the Rosh about the Rambam, and R' Yehoshua Falk (the Sma) about the Shulchan Aruch without its commentaries ([EJ, Codification of Law](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/codification-law)).

The ladder in the last section reconciles the two: you walk from the sugya to the code and back, so you know both *what* the halakha is and *why*.

## Where to go: a Gemara question, routed by type

Diagnose the kind of question first, then pick the genre that answers it. Want someone to *explain* Rashi or Tosafot? Open an explainer (Maharsha, Maharam). Already understand and want the *problem you missed*? Open a question-raiser (Pnei Yehoshua, R' Akiva Eiger). Want the *nature of the law*? Start with Encyclopedia Talmudit, then Ketzot, R' Chaim or R' Shimon.

| # | Your question | Look first | Then | Deeper / specialist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What does this word or phrase mean? | Rashi; Steinsaltz, Koren or Schottenstein | Jastrow; the Aruch or Aruch HaShalem for loanwords | Technical terms (תיובתא, קשיא, איבעיא): Yad Malachi; Halichot Olam; Mevo HaTalmud (back of Berakhot) |
| 2 | The text looks corrupt | The margin: Hagahot HaBach, Gilyon HaShas, Hagahot HaGra; Maharshal, Rashash | A Rishon quoting a different reading; Rabbeinu Chananel or the Rif for a pre-Rashi text | Hachi Garsinan (manuscripts, Genizah, early prints); Dikdukei Soferim where it exists |
| 3 | I don't follow the flow of the sugya | Rashi, then Steinsaltz's running explanation | Maharam Lublin; Maharsha; the Meiri for a clean summary | Sephardi iyun: why is each word needed, what wrong reasoning does it block, where is the heart of the answer? Write it out |
| 4 | A question on Rashi | Maharsha | Maharam Lublin, Maharam Schiff, Rashash | Tosafot and the Rishonim who read differently (Ramban, Rashba, Ritva); Pnei Yehoshua; Tzlach |
| 5 | A question on Tosafot | Maharsha | Tosafot HaRosh; Maharam; Gilyon HaShas | Pnei Yehoshua; R' Akiva Eiger; Hafla'ah (Ketubot) and Makneh (Kiddushin); Aruch LaNer (his tractates) |
| 6 | A contradiction with a sugya elsewhere | Tosafot here and at the parallel; Mesoret HaShas; Gilyon HaShas | R' Akiva Eiger; Pnei Yehoshua; Tzlach | Encyclopedia Talmudit (gathers every locus); lomdus works that reconcile with *tzvei dinim* or a *chakira* |
| 7 | How do the other Rishonim read this? | Ramban, Rashba, Ritva, Ran — whichever your tractate has | The Meiri as a map of positions; Ra'ah, Yad Ramah, Ri Migash by tractate | Shita Mekubetzet for lost Rishonim; Otzar Mefarshei HaTalmud; Otzar HaGeonim |
| 8 | What is the nature of this law? (gavra/cheftza, ma'aseh/kiyum) | Encyclopedia Talmudit entry | Ketzot + Netivot (monetary and procedural mechanisms); Avnei Miluim (marriage and divorce); Shev Shema'tata (doubt, presumption) | R' Chaim on the Rambam; Sha'arei Yosher; Kovetz Shiurim; Birkat Shmuel; Kehillot Yaakov; Avi Ezri; the Chazon Ish as a Gemara-first check |
| 9 | Why does the Rambam rule this way? | Kesef Mishneh, Maggid Mishneh; the Raavad's gloss | Lechem Mishneh; Mishneh LaMelech | R' Chaim; Or Sameach; Even HaAzel; Avi Ezri |
| 10 | Where does this land in halakha? | Ein Mishpat on the page | Rif with Ran or Nimukei Yosef; the Rosh | The halakha ladder in the next section |
| 11 | When do we rule like Abaye or Rava, or like the later Amora? | Yad Malachi | Halichot Olam; Mevo HaTalmud | Sdei Chemed |
| 12 | An aggadah: what does it say? | Rashi; Maharsha, Chiddushei Aggadot | Ein Yaakov: Etz Yosef, Iyun Yaakov, HaKotev; Ben Yehoyada | Chida's Petach Einayim; the Rashba's Perush HaHaggadot |
| 13 | An aggadah: what is the big idea? | Anaf Yosef; Ben Yehoyada's *remez* layer | Maharal — Chiddushei Aggadot, then his thematic book (Netzach Yisrael for exile, Netivot Olam for character) | Ein Ayah (Berakhot, Shabbat); R' Tzadok; Pachad Yitzchak; Fraenkel or Rubenstein for structure |
| 14 | Is there a Yerushalmi parallel? | Mesoret HaShas; Rabbeinu Chananel | Pnei Moshe (פני משה) | Korban HaEdah (קרבן העדה); the Gra's Yerushalmi emendations |
| 15 | How was the Talmud composed and transmitted? | Iggeret Rav Sherira Gaon (אגרת רב שרירא גאון) | Maharatz Chajes's Mevo HaTalmud | Rabbinovicz on the printings; Hachi Garsinan |

*The routing is a synthesis from the documented function of each work; it is not a published guide. Many Acharonim cover only some tractates, so check what is printed at the back of your volume first.*

**A typical iyun session on one sugya** (reconstructed; no single guide states it):

1. Read the Gemara several times with Rashi.
2. Read Tosafot.
3. Read the Rishonim your yeshiva uses for that tractate — typically Rashba, Ritva, Ran, Ramban and Rosh — plus the Rif with the Ran or Nimukei Yosef, and the Rambam with Maggid Mishneh or Kesef Mishneh.
4. Take the difficulties to the Maharsha, Pnei Yehoshua and R' Akiva Eiger.
5. Where the topic is a legal mechanism, open Ketzot and Netivot.
6. Finish with the lomdus sources your rosh yeshiva favors.

The Sephardi variant front-loads the Canpanton-style close reading of Gemara, Rashi and Tosafot, turns to Beit Yosef for halakha, and treats Litvish lomdus as optional and later. For beki'ut, Gemara with Rashi and a glance at the margin is the whole kit.

## Where to go: from Gemara to halakha, step by step

The answer to "the Rif, the Rambam, the Beit Yosef or the Shulchan Aruch?" is all of them, in order, each for a different job. Karo himself describes the long road — the sugya with Rashi and Tosafot, then the Rif, Rosh, Ran and Mordechai, then the Rambam, the other poskim and the responsa — calls it endless, and says he wrote Beit Yosef to shortcut it ([Sefaria, Beit Yosef introduction](https://www.sefaria.org/Beit_Yosef,_Introduction)).

| Step | Open | What you learn there | Ashkenazi variant | Sephardi variant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The sugya with Rashi and Tosafot | The Talmudic data and its main readings | Same | Same, with Canpanton-style precision |
| 2 | Ein Mishpat Ner Mitzvah, on the page | Where the law sits in the Rambam, SMaG, Tur and Shulchan Aruch | Same | Same |
| 3 | Rif (with Ran or Nimukei Yosef) and Rosh, at the back of the volume | What survived the sugya as law, and what the Tosafist reading changes | Weight the Rosh and the Mordechai | Weight the Rif |
| 4 | Rambam + Maggid Mishneh / Kesef Mishneh + the Raavad | One clean ruling, its source, and the main challenge | Add Hagahot Maimoniyot | Central — the Rambam is Maran's usual partner. For Yemenite Dor De'ah and Baladi, often the end point (Kafih's edition) |
| 5 | Tur, same siman | The practical category and the map of Rishonic views | Same | Same |
| 6 | Beit Yosef | Each view traced to the sugya; Karo's ruling by the three pillars | Then Darkei Moshe, the Bach, Prisha/Drisha for the Ashkenazi resolution | Beit Yosef is itself the binding reasoning (for R' Shalom Messas, only the Shulchan Aruch and Beit Yosef bind) |
| 7 | Shulchan Aruch + Rema | The operative norm | The Rema's הגה is your default | The Mechaber's text is your default |
| 8 | The primary nosei kelim | How the norm was read and adjusted | OC: Magen Avraham, Taz. YD: Shach, Taz. EH: Beit Shmuel, Chelkat Mechokek. CM: Sma, Shach. Plus the Gra | The Chida's Birkei Yosef; Knesset HaGedolah; Pri Chadash (cited, not overriding Maran) |
| 9 | Explainers and digests — as tools only | A way into the voices | Pri Megadim, Machatzit HaShekel; Be'er Heitev, Sha'arei Teshuvah, Pitchei Teshuvah | Kaf HaChaim as an anthology; regional commentaries |
| 10 | Your community's synthesizing code | The practical "last word" as lived today | Mishnah Berurah (OC); Aruch HaShulchan where the MB is silent or in some circles; Shulchan Aruch HaRav (Chabad); Chazon Ish (Bnei Brak); Peninei Halakha (Religious Zionist) | By school: Yalkut Yosef / Halacha Berura (Rav Ovadia); Ben Ish Chai / Kaf HaChaim / Or LeTzion (Iraqi and Kabbalah-weighted); Moroccan poskim; Mekor Chaim (Uziel/HaLevi); Shulchan Aruch HaMekutzar (Yemenite Baladi) |
| 11 | Responsa and contemporary poskim | New cases and your community's living authority | Igrot Moshe; R' Shlomo Zalman Auerbach; R' Elyashiv; Shevet HaLevi; Shemirat Shabbat KeHilchata for Shabbat | Yabia Omer, Yechaveh Da'at; Or LeTzion; R' Mordechai Eliyahu; Shemesh uMagen; Aseh Lecha Rav |
| 12 | Your rav | The actual ruling for your case | Required | Required |

*Steps 1–7 follow Karo's own description and the educator guides linked here; the community assignments in steps 8–11 are synthesized from the sections above.* An Israeli guide proposes the same order for yeshiva halakha study — sugya, the Rishonim cited in Beit Yosef, Tur and Shulchan Aruch, nosei kelim, then the Acharonim via the Mishnah Berurah or Pitchei Teshuvah ([Tzarich Iyun](https://iyun.org.il/sedersheni/halacha/)). The Chief Rabbinate's semicha exams are reportedly built on "Tur, Beit Yosef, Shulchan Aruch with commentaries" for Shabbat, kashrut and niddah; that comes from an exam-prep site, so check the exact simanim before relying on it ([limd.co.il](https://www.limd.co.il/t/%D7%90%D7%99%D7%96%D7%95-%D7%91%D7%97%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%91%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%93%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%AA-%D7%9B%D7%93%D7%99-%D7%9C%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%9C-%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%94/)).

**The short route when time is limited:** Gemara → Tur → Beit Yosef → Shulchan Aruch + Rema → one primary nosei keli → your community's code. Beit Yosef already contains the Rif, Rosh and Rambam with their commentators for that siman. An Ashkenazi guide recommends the same start and warns against piling up so many opinions that you end up confused ([Din Online](https://dinonline.org/2021/08/08/how-to-approach-learning-tur-shulchan-aruch/)).

Step 12 is not a formality. This ladder builds your understanding of *why* the halakha is what it is; it does not make you the one who issues the psak.

## Glossary, disputed dates, sources

| Term | Hebrew | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perush | פירוש | Running explanation of the wording (Rashi's genre) |
| Tosafot | תוספות | Dialectical glosses that harmonize all of Shas; originally glosses on Rashi (the *kuntres*) |
| Chiddushim | חידושים | Analytic novellae on a sugya (Ramban, Rashba, Ritva, Ran; later the Acharonim) |
| Mefarshim | מפרשים | Explainers of an earlier commentary (Maharsha on Tosafot, Pri Megadim on the Magen Avraham) |
| Pilpul | פלפול | Historically, 16th-century Polish dialectic of ingenious distinctions; used loosely today for any sharp analysis |
| Lomdus | למדנות | Conceptual analysis: definitions, *chakirot*, *tzvei dinim* |
| Chakira | חקירה | A root conceptual alternative that explains a series of disputes |
| Tzvei dinim | תרי דיני | "Two laws": resolving a contradiction by finding two distinct legal elements |
| Gavra / cheftza | גברא / חפצא | An obligation on the person vs. a status of the object |
| Iyun / beki'ut | עיון / בקיאות | Depth study / breadth study |
| Girsa | גרסא | A textual reading; ה"ג (הכי גרסינן) marks a proposed one |
| Sefer halakhot | ספר הלכות | Digest of the Gemara in its own words (Rif, Rosh) |
| Sefer pesakim | ספר פסקים | Code of bare rulings without sources (Rambam, Shulchan Aruch) |
| Hasagot | השגות | Critical glosses (the Raavad on the Rambam) |
| Hagahah / Mapah | הגהה / המפה | A gloss inside the code's text; the Rema's glosses on the Shulchan Aruch |
| Nosei kelim | נושאי כלים | "Armor-bearers": the standard commentaries printed around a code |
| Mar'eh mekomot | מראה מקומות | Source references (Be'er HaGolah, Ein Mishpat) |
| Rishonim / Acharonim | ראשונים / אחרונים | Authorities before / after the Shulchan Aruch — roughly 11th–15th c. vs. 16th c. on |
| Amudei hahora'ah | עמודי ההוראה | Karo's three pillars: Rif, Rambam, Rosh |
| Maran / the Mechaber | מרן / המחבר | R' Yosef Karo |
| Kabbalat hora'ot Maran | קבלת הוראות מרן | Sephardi acceptance of Karo's rulings as binding, even against a majority |
| Mara de-atra | מרא דאתרא | The recognized authority of a place |
| Hilkheta kebatrai | הלכתא כבתראי | The law follows the later authorities (from Abaye and Rava on) |
| Safek berachot lehakel | ספק ברכות להקל | When a blessing is in doubt, omit it |
| Koach de-hetera adif | כחא דהיתרא עדיף | A well-founded leniency is preferred |
| Posek acharon | פוסק אחרון | The latest decisor, treated as the "last word" |

### Where sources disagree

- **Shulchan Aruch:** completed 1563 (EJ, Sefaria) or 1558 (Hebrew Wikipedia); first printed Venice 1564–65. The Rema's glosses first printed Kraków 1569–71 in most sources, 1578 in one. The Rema died 1572, or 1573.
- **Rishonim dates vary:** the Ran's birth (1290 or c. 1320); the Ritva (c. 1250/60 to 1314/1330, and some works printed under his name are not his); the Meiri (1249–1315 in most sources).
- **Later dates:** Mishnah Berurah volume years differ by a year between printings; the Ben Ish Chai was born 1834 or 1835; R' Yaakov Chaim Sofer 1869 or 1870.
- **Attributions:** "Rabbeinu Gershom" on the page is the Mainz *Kuntres Magenza*; the "Rashi" on Nedarim and Nazir is not Rashi's. Which Tosafot collection is printed on which tractate is disputed among the reference works.
- **Volume counts** for Yalkut Yosef and Halacha Berura vary with edition and year.
